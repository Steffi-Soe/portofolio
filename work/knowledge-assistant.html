Case study · 2026

A cited-answers assistant on one 8 GB laptop GPU

The ACS Knowledge Assistant answers IT staff questions from internal policies, SOPs and budget data. Every answer points to its source, and it says so when the answer isn't in the documents. Nothing leaves the company.

My role
Built it end to end
When
Jul – Sep 2026
Status
In use since Aug 2026
Built with
Python, FastAPI, Qdrant, Ollama, Docling, DuckDB, React

The problem

The IT department keeps its policies, SOPs, work instructions, forms and budget sheets in SharePoint. Finding one answer meant opening several documents, and it took about eight minutes. Most people simply asked someone in IT.

Constraints

  • The data stays in the company. No cloud AI services, so every model runs locally.
  • One laptop GPU. An RTX 4060 with 8 GB of memory has to run search, the language model and everything else.
  • Two languages, messy files. Documents mix Indonesian and English, and some are scanned PDFs.
  • Not everyone sees everything. Access depends on department, role and clearance.

How it works

Questions take one of two lanes. Questions about documents go through search and come back as a cited answer. Questions about numbers go to a small SQL engine, so the figure is calculated rather than written by the model.

Decisions I made

  • Hybrid search instead of embeddings alone. People search by meaning, but also by exact form codes and names, in two languages. Qdrant combines multilingual-e5 embeddings with BM25 keyword search and merges the two rankings.
  • Access control inside the search. The filter runs before documents are retrieved, so a document you can't see never reaches the model. Hiding it in the interface wouldn't be enough.
  • Refusing is a feature. When the sources don't support an answer, it says so and points to the IT helpdesk, instead of guessing.
  • I measured it with a fixed question set. 88 questions: 63 it should answer, 22 it should refuse, and 3 partial cases. Every change is run against the same set, three times.

What moved the numbers

Three changes I measured along the way. Two of them had nothing to do with the language model.

What didn't work

  • Letting a model grade the answers. I tried using a 7B model to judge answer quality. Its scores weren't consistent enough to trust, so I dropped it and scored retrieval and citations directly against the question set.
  • More stages, more waiting. I built a reranker and a fact-checking step, but they're switched off for now.
  • Speed is about the model, not the search. Finding the passages takes about 0.16 seconds. Nearly all of the wait is the language model writing the answer, and on a laptop GPU the slowest answers still take around half a minute.

Results

  • In use since August 2026, and handed over to the IT team on 1 September.
  • On the 88-question set (average of three runs): the right document is cited 85.7% of the time, it makes the right call on answering or declining 90.6% of the time, and the median answer takes 6.5 seconds.
  • About 4.8 minutes saved per question compared with searching by hand.

What I'd do next

Move it from my laptop to a dedicated server, which would also make the slow answers faster. Then bring the reranker back and measure whether it's worth the extra wait.
